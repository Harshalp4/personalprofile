An AI agent platform that runs marketing and sales for local service businesses

Local service businesses lose repeat customers, can’t tell which ads bring orders, and have no time to make content. I designed and built Revora: a set of AI agents that handle that work, with a person approving every action before it reaches a customer.

The problem

A neighbourhood laundry business has the same marketing problems as most local services. Customers order once or twice and drift away. Ads bring WhatsApp chats, but nobody can say which chats turned into orders. Posting regularly on Instagram or YouTube needs time the owner doesn’t have.

Off-the-shelf tools each solve one slice: a WhatsApp inbox here, a scheduling tool there, an ads dashboard somewhere else. None of them connect a WhatsApp conversation to the order it produced, and none of them are safe to hand to an AI without a person checking the output.

What made it hard

  • AI must never act alone. A wrong price or promise sent to a customer costs real money and trust, so every customer-facing action had to be reviewable.
  • Small-business budget. No paid cloud services. The whole platform had to run on one machine with Docker Compose.
  • Attribution across channels. A click-to-WhatsApp ad, a chat, a human reply and an order in another system all had to link up.
  • Provider risk. AI model prices and quality change monthly, so no feature could depend on one vendor.

What I built

Revora is a .NET 10 modular monolith with an Angular 22 portal, PostgreSQL 18 and Redis. Background work runs on Hangfire with a Postgres outbox instead of a paid message broker, which keeps it self-hosted without losing reliable delivery.

  • Revenue loop first. I built retention reminders, the WhatsApp sales agent and order attribution before any content generation, because those earn money soonest.
  • WhatsApp sales agent in shadow mode. Inbound messages become leads, keeping the ad’s click id. The agent drafts a reply, a person sends or edits it, and edits are stored against the draft so the agent can be judged and improved. Escalation keywords hand a chat to a person before any model is called.
  • Closed-loop attribution. Every order gets an attribution record (lead, ad, click, retention action, first order or not) and is sent to the Meta Conversions API, so ad spend is judged on orders, not clicks.
  • Segmentation and retention. Customers are segmented every night by their own ordering rhythm (new, regular, at risk, dormant, VIP), and only consented customers receive reminders, outside quiet hours.
  • Content and ads agents. Research and competitor agents propose opportunities; a campaign agent plans offers with test and control locations; a creative director agent plans scenes rendered with image, video and voice models plus FFmpeg. Deterministic checks block wrong prices and unverifiable claims before anything reaches approval.
  • Provider-agnostic AI layer. A provider router sits in front of OpenAI and Anthropic models, with per-run cost tracking and a budget guard.

How it fits together

  1. Customer dataOrders and customers mirrored from the business’s own system or CSV
  2. AgentsSales, retention, research, campaign, content, creative, reviews and an AI CMO
  3. Approval CentreA person approves, edits or rejects every message, post and budget change
  4. ChannelsWhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube and Google Business Profile
  5. AttributionOrders tied back to the lead, ad and click, and reported to Meta

Security and control

  • Secrets encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a master key and always returned masked.
  • Seven roles (super admin, tenant admin, marketing manager, approver, content creator, analyst, viewer) with JWT sessions.
  • Every settings change written to an audit log; signed webhooks for WhatsApp and the order system.

Where it stands

All eight build sprints are complete and the first tenant, a laundry chain in Navi Mumbai, is being onboarded. Results from live customers will be added here once there is enough data to report honestly.

The design already shows the main benefit: the owner reviews a short queue of ready-made replies, reminders and posts each day instead of producing them, and every rupee of ad spend can be traced to the orders it produced.
