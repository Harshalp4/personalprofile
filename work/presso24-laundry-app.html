Building a pickup-and-delivery laundry service from booking to doorstep

Presso24 needed a complete system for a new laundry service in Navi Mumbai: a customer app to book pickups, an app for riders and facility staff, and an admin portal. As technology partner, I designed and built all of it, keeping running costs close to zero.

The problem

Laundry and dry cleaning in most neighbourhoods still runs on phone calls and paper slips. Customers don’t know when a rider will come, what a garment will cost, or when it will be back. The business can’t see which orders are late or which customers have stopped ordering.

Presso24 wanted to launch a service that felt as simple as ordering food: pick a slot, hand over the bag, track it, get it back. That meant building every piece, not just a customer app.

What made it hard

  • Startup budget. Hosting, maps, messaging and storage all had to cost little or nothing until order volume justified more.
  • Physical handovers. Garments change hands twice (pickup and delivery), so each handover needed proof, and item counts had to match.
  • Weak mobile networks. Riders upload photos from building lobbies and basements with poor signal.
  • One person in many roles. In a small operation the same person may ride, process orders and manage the facility.

What I built

  • Customer app (Flutter, Android and iOS). Slot booking per pincode, per-garment pricing, live order tracking, rewards (coins, referrals, scratch cards, streaks) and promo codes.
  • AI booking. Customers can describe their order in text, voice or a photo, and an Anthropic Claude model turns it into a draft booking.
  • Operations app (Flutter). Rider, facility and operator modules, with OTP-verified pickup and delivery. Pickup is blocked when item counts don’t match, and photo uploads queue offline until the network returns.
  • Automated dispatch. A background worker reminds riders 30, 15 and 5 minutes before a slot, auto-assigns when due and escalates missed pickups.
  • Admin portal (Angular). Prices for every service and garment, pickup slots, service zones, scheduled banners and reports.
  • .NET 10 API. Minimal APIs with EF Core on PostgreSQL, SignalR for live updates, Razorpay with signature-checked webhooks, SMS and email OTP, and Google and Apple sign-in.
  • Low running cost. Docker hosting, OpenStreetMap tiles instead of a paid maps API, and Firebase for push notifications.

How it fits together

  5. DeliveryOut for delivery, OTP check at the door, rewards credited

Fixing what audits found

I ran my own audits of the system and fixed what they found: the operations app was showing some failed requests as successful, role checks were missing on facility endpoints, and a public configuration endpoint exposed more than it should. Role policies, an allowlisted configuration endpoint and correct error handling are now in place.

Where it stands

  • The customer app is live on Google Play, with the operations app in daily use by the team.
  • The full order lifecycle runs in software: ten tracked order states, from booking to doorstep, with every handover verified.
  • Ad installs are attributed to first orders without collecting personal data, so marketing spend can be judged on real orders.
  • Older orders taken before the app were imported, so the full customer history is in one place.
